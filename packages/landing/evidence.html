Conditions of sale

How a Fuin auction runs

Zcash has no contracts, so the auction service applies these rules. Everything it decides from can be re-read from the chain with the published key.

  1. Bound terms. The auction id is a hash of every term on the card: close, reveal window, reserve, deposit, keys and seller. Every deposit memo and the proof carry it, so no term can change after the bids. Sales 001 and 002 predate this; the audit flags them as legacy. Sale 003 is bound.

  2. Commit. A bidder picks a bid and a random 248-bit salt and computes Poseidon(auction, bid, salt).

  3. Deposit. It sends exactly the auction's deposit to the auction address, shielded, with memo sba1:<auction>:<commitment>. All deposits are equal, so the amount says nothing.

  4. Close. Deposits mined after the close height are late. A copied commitment is a duplicate; a wrong amount or a non-bid memo is not a bid. Only the first 8 revealed valid deposits compete (the proof has 8 slots); a deposit nobody reveals takes no slot, and later revealed ones are refunded.

  5. Reveal. After the close, each bidder sends a 1-zat shielded note to the auction address. Its memo is the bid, salt and refund address sealed to the auction's reveal key, so the public viewing key shows only which deposit it opens and when. A reveal outside the window, one that does not decrypt, or one whose opening does not match the deposit it names is refused.

  6. Prove. The MaxBid(8) Groth16 proof shows the opened slot holds the highest bid and meets the reserve. Slots run in chain order, so the earlier deposit wins a tie; within one block that is the lower txid. Every other bid stays private.

  7. Tie it to the chain. The proof's slots must be the first 8 revealed valid deposits in chain order, any unused slot must be empty, and the winner must be a real deposit. A valid deposit left out has to be listed as unrevealed.

  8. Settle. One shielded transaction pays the seller the winning bid and refunds every losing bid in full; any other revealed deposit (late, duplicate, wrong amount, over capacity) comes back minus its own 5,000 zat output fee. The winner gets its deposit back minus the bid and a 60,000 zat fee budget. An unrevealed deposit goes to the seller, not the auctioneer.

  9. No sale. If nothing valid is revealed, or the highest revealed bid is below the reserve, there is no sale: every revealed deposit is refunded minus its output fee and unrevealed ones go to the seller. A proof with reserve 0 shows the highest bid, and the audit checks it is below the reserve.

  10. Dispute. For a set number of blocks after the settlement, a bidder whose valid reveal was ignored, or who was underpaid, can file a signed complaint anchored on chain. The audit checks it against the chain and names the failure. Filing one makes that bidder's own bid public.

What is new here

Not the first sealed-bid ZK auction

zkSNARKs ran a blind-bid ZEC auction in September 2026 (reported: 16,971 bids, cleared at 1.5 ZEC, settled through NEAR Intents), and Ethereum has many commit-reveal auctions with ZK proofs. What Fuin adds:

  1. The deposits are the public inputs. Every bid is an equal shielded deposit whose memo is the commitment the MaxBid proof is about, so the proof is tied to exactly the deposits on chain: no phantom bid, none left out.
  2. Reveals are sealed inside shielded memos. The chain timestamps each one against the reveal window, and a dropped reveal is visible to anyone with the viewing key, without exposing the bid.
  3. Complaints and audit need no trust. A bidder's signed complaint is anchored on chain, and the audit, with only the viewing key, proves the record is complete: no hidden deposit, no hidden reveal.

Sources: Crypto Briefing · NEAR Intents blog · Nightash

What Fuin does not do

Read this before you bid.

The auctioneer learns every bid

It decrypts each opening at the reveal and is trusted to settle. It cannot prove a winner the bids do not support, or leave a deposit or a reveal note out of its record without failing the audit. The converse needs care: anyone can pay the auction address after the auctioneer's scan, so a record says how far it scanned and the audit reports later notes, which are late and cannot change the outcome, instead of failing. Sales 001 and 002 predate bound terms, so their close and reveal window come from the record: an auctioneer could have moved them. Sale 003's are bound. Nothing forces it to settle.

Reveals cost a transaction

Each bidder sends one more shielded note (about 10,000 zat in fees), and the public key shows which deposits were revealed and when. Sale 001 predates the reveal channel: one script held every opening.

Complaints publish

Filing one makes that bidder's bid public. Adding a refund key makes every payment to that refund wallet readable: Sale 002's complaint carries the winner's whole demo-wallet key; Sale 003's carries none.

Single-party trusted setup

Whoever ran the Groth16 setup could forge proofs. A multi-party ceremony with outside contributors would remove that trust; none has been run.

The audit trusts its lightwalletd

The completeness check reads blocks and heights from one lightwalletd (testnet.zec.rocks by default). A server that hid a transaction would mislead it; run the audit against a second server, or your own node, to compare.

Payments need a recipient key

The auction's key sees none of the settlement's outputs. Without the seller's viewing key, or a complaint carrying a refund key, the audit ends chain checks pass; payments not checked, not all checks pass.

Ties go to the lower txid

Within one block the earlier slot is the lexically lower txid, not the transaction mined first, so a bidder could grind its txid to win a same-block tie.

Testnet only, and not the first

Sealed-bid auctions with zero-knowledge proofs have prior art; what Fuin adds is listed above.
